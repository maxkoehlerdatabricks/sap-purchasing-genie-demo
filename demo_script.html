A UI-first demo walkthrough on Databricks.

1 · The hook — a $2M question 1 min

"Imagine one part on your assembly line comes from a single supplier — and that supplier just got flagged for delivery risk. If they slip, the line stops. Today, finding those parts means waiting on an SAP specialist. Let me show you how a purchasing analyst answers that in seconds, in plain English, on Databricks."
Keep this framing throughout: we're not showing a database, we're removing the dependency on SAP experts for everyday sourcing-risk questions.

2 · Catalog Explorer — the governed data model 4 min

Start in the Databricks UI left nav → Catalog.

  1. CLICK Catalog → serverless_stable_7qzrfp_catalog → purchasing_demo. Show the list of tables.
    "We simulated a realistic SAP purchasing landscape — 13 raw SAP tables — and consolidated them into 5 clean, analysis-ready 'gold' tables."
  2. CLICK the SOURCING table → Overview tab.
    The table description and the column comments — every column is documented in business language (e.g. "IS_VALID_SOURCE = a valid, non-blocked source").
    "This is the heart of the model — which vendors are approved to supply which material at which plant, plus their quota split."
  3. CLICK the Sample data tab, then the Details / Relationships view.
    The primary key and the foreign key to MATERIAL. These constraints aren't just documentation — Genie uses them to join correctly.
  4. CLICK into Functions in the schema. Open e.g. materials_without_multisourcing.
    These are governed, parameterized SQL functions — "trusted assets." A business rule like "fewer than 2 valid vendors" is defined once, centrally, and reused everywhere — including by Genie as a certified answer.
  5. CLICK a metric view such as mv_delivery_performance and open its definition.
    "KPIs like on-time-delivery rate are defined once as a metric view, so every dashboard, Genie space, and app computes them identically. No metric drift."
Don't dwell on SQL here. The message is governance: documented tables, keys, certified functions, and consistent metrics — the foundation that makes the AI layer trustworthy.

3 · Genie: Sourcing & Supplier Risk 5 min

Left nav → Genie → open "SAP Purchasing — Sourcing & Supplier Risk."

  1. SHOW the space landing page.
    The sample questions down the side and the attached tables. Mention the space was configured with instructions (domain rules, join hints, example queries, and certified functions) — that's why answers are accurate.
  2. TYPE Which materials have no multi-sourcing strategy?
    When the answer returns, expand "Show generated code." Genie called our certified function materials_without_multisourcing(min_vendors => 2) — not hand-written SQL.
    "Notice it didn't guess. It used the trusted, governed definition of multi-sourcing we saw in the catalog. That's the difference between a demo and something you'd trust in production."
  3. TYPE the flagship question: Which materials are single-sourced and their sole supplier has a delivery-risk flag?
    The result — the highest-priority supply-continuity risks, with a risk score per material.
    "This is the $2M question from the start. Seconds, in plain English."
  4. TYPE a follow-up to show conversational memory: Of those, which are at plant 1030?
    Genie keeps context and refines the previous answer.
  5. TYPE a governance question: Which suppliers hold a purchasing block but still have active sourcing records?
    "A classic control gap — a blocked vendor that's still sourceable. The analyst finds it without opening a ticket."
If someone asks "how does it know what 'valid vendor' means?" — that's the instructions. Open the space's Instructions panel and show the text rules and the certified functions attached.

4 · Genie: Purchase Order Analytics 3 min

Open the second space — "SAP Purchasing — Purchase Order Analytics."

  1. TYPE What is the on-time delivery rate per supplier over the last 12 months?
    Again, "Show generated code" reveals it called otd_rate_per_supplier(12).
  2. TYPE Which materials had purchase orders placed with a supplier despite an active delivery-risk flag at order date?
    "This needs time-aware logic — was the risk active on the day we ordered? That rule is baked into the model, so the analyst doesn't have to reason about it."
  3. TYPE What is the net-price variance across vendors for the same material? — a quick sourcing-savings angle.
"Two focused spaces, each an expert in its domain. But I don't want a business user to pick the right space — so we put a supervisor in front."

5 · Agent Bricks — the Multi-Agent Supervisor 3 min

Left nav → Agents (Agent Bricks) → open "sap_purchasing_supervisor."

  1. SHOW the supervisor's config.
    The two agents it orchestrates (Sourcing & Supplier Risk, Purchase Order Analytics) and the routing instructions.
  2. CLICK the built-in playground / test panel. Ask a cross-domain question: Do we have single-sourced risky materials that we also placed purchase orders for recently?
    The trace: the supervisor routes to the right agent(s), each runs its Genie space, and the answer is combined — one endpoint, both domains.
    "The business user just asks. The supervisor decides who answers. This is the single brain the app will talk to."
  3. SHOW (optional) the serving endpoint under Serving — it's deployed and queryable like any model endpoint.

6 · The App — one screen for the business 4 min

Left nav → Apps → open "sap-purchasing-app."

  1. SHOW the Visualizations tab.
    The KPI cards up top — materials without multi-sourcing, single-sourced & risky, suppliers at delivery risk, overall on-time rate — quantify the risk at a glance. Then the four charts (sourcing coverage, risk by category, worst OTD suppliers, price spread).
    "Every number here comes from the same governed metric views and functions we saw in the catalog — the app isn't its own source of truth, it's a window onto the governed layer."
  2. CLICK the Chat (MAS) tab. Click a sample question chip, or type Which suppliers have the worst on-time delivery?
    The answer streams back with a "routed to …" badge showing which agent the supervisor picked.
    "Same intelligence as Genie, embedded where the business already works — behind our own branding, our own screen."
  3. SAY the punchline.
    "Charts for the overview, chat for the follow-up — and both are powered by the exact same governed data, functions, and agents. Nothing is bolted on."

7 · Close — trust & what it took 1 min

"To make natural language trustworthy, we grounded it: documented tables with keys, KPIs defined once as metric views, business rules as certified functions, and a benchmark that scores the agent's answers against known-good SQL — it scored 100% on our question set."
The whole thing is a set of manually-runnable notebooks, parameterized once, reproducible on a blank workspace — no black boxes.
If asked "can we point this at our real SAP data?" — yes: swap the simulated raw tables for your ingested SAP tables; the gold workflows, functions, metric views, Genie spaces, supervisor and app all sit on top unchanged.
